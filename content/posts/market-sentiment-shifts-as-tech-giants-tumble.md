The Indian stock market closed on a sour note today, with the benchmark indices plunging due to a sharp decline in tech giants. The Nifty 50 index plummeted by 1.5% to 23,419.50, while the Sensex fell by 1.3% to 77,311.50.

**TCS: A Key Driver of the Decline**

Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) was one of the top losers, with its stock price falling by 1.9% to ₹3,951.90. The company's net profit fell by 4.5% in the January-March quarter, missing analyst estimates. The decline in TCS's stock price dragged down the entire IT sector, with Infosys and Wipro also posting losses. Infosys, in particular, saw its stock price drop to ₹1,549, down 1.2% from its previous close.

**Reliance Industries: A Mixed Bag**

Reliance Industries, on the other hand, saw its stock price fall by 1.1% to ₹1,279. The company's mixed quarterly results, with a 5% increase in revenue but a 2% decline in net profit, led to a cautious response from investors. However, the stock's long-term prospects remain strong, driven by its diversified business portfolio and growing presence in the renewable energy sector.

**HDFC Bank: A Surprise Loser**

HDFC Bank, one of the largest private sector banks in India, was also among the top losers, with its stock price falling by 1.4% to ₹819. The bank's quarterly results, which showed a 15% increase in net profit, were largely in line with analyst estimates. However, the stock's valuation multiples, which have been rich of late, may have led to some profit-taking by investors.

**Other Losers**

Other notable losers included Tata Motors, which fell by 1.6% to ₹979, and Hindalco, which declined by 1.3% to ₹959. Tata Steel, which has been on a tear of late, also saw its stock price fall by 1.2% to ₹179, after the company's recent 10:1 stock split.

**A Silver Lining**

Despite the sharp decline in the market, there were some bright spots. State Bank of India (SBIN), for example, saw its stock price rise by 0.5% to ₹549, driven by its strong quarterly results and improving asset quality. The bank's net profit increased by 20% in the January-March quarter, beating analyst estimates.
